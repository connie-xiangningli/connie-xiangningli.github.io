Heritage Language Education at a Crossroads: A Systematic Review of two Decades of Research in the UK (2004–2024)

Li, X., Zhou, Y., & Liu, Y. · Research Synthesis in Applied Linguistics 2(1), 258–289 · DOI ↗

Abstract at a glance

This review brings together 83 empirical studies of heritage-language education in the UK from 2004 to 2024. It examines research methods, educational and social outcomes, key debates, and remaining gaps. The authors propose directions for research and practice that attend to language legitimacy, life courses, participant voice, and sustainable systems of support.

Mind the Gap: English Teachers’ Perceptions and Practices of Integrating Social Media into Language Classrooms in China

Li, X., & Liu, Y. · System 125, 103434 · DOI ↗

Abstract at a glance

Drawing on questionnaires, instructional documents, and interviews with English teachers in China, this mixed methods study compares teachers’ views of social media with their classroom practice. It identifies a substantial gap between favourable perceptions and more limited use, and discusses how individual, school, and wider social conditions shape that gap.

A systematic review of the first year of publications on ChatGPT and language education: Examining research on ChatGPT’s use in language learning and teaching

Li, B., Lowell, V. L., Wang, C., & Li, X. · Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence 7, 100266 · DOI ↗

Abstract at a glance

This systematic review maps 36 studies published during ChatGPT’s first year in language education. It surveys methods, settings, topics, and disciplinary spread. The review finds a concentration of empirical work in higher education and on ethical questions, while identifying needs for longer studies, broader contexts, and closer attention to feedback quality and stakeholder views.

Pedagogical Models for Plagiarism-Free Learning in Academia

Li, X., Lam, S. W., Zhang, Y., & Chu, S. K. W. · Book chapter in Information Literacy Education of Higher Education in Asian Countries, pp. 85–95 · DOI ↗

Abstract at a glance

This chapter presents two teaching models designed to help students avoid plagiarism: a sequence for secondary students covering understanding, paraphrasing, citation, and originality checks, and an online writing support system for undergraduates. It draws on evaluations of these approaches to discuss their use in academic integrity education.
