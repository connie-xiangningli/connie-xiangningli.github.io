Multilingualism
through an ecological lens.

I study multilingualism in education and everyday life, with work on heritage-language education, migration, and digital spaces. An ecological perspective often guides how I connect people, languages, and their environments. My doctoral research centres on conceptualising multilingual well-being and developing it as a construct.

About

Languages, places, and the paths between them ↗

I grew up in Chongqing, China, with Chongqing dialect and Mandarin around me. English is now part of my everyday and academic life too: I speak all three fluently, and I am learning French. As my studies and work have taken me between China and the UK, I have become more curious about how languages move with people—and how they are learned, lived, and experienced across places and communities.

My early research focused on technology in language education. Over time, my interests broadened to heritage-language education, migration, and multilingual well-being. My professional experience in London, in consultancy and higher education, also informs how I think about research in relation to the institutions and people around it.

At Cambridge, I am a member of Queens’ College, part of CRiCLE-Net in the Faculty of Education, and connected with Cambridge Language Sciences. My PhD research is supervised by Professor Yongcan Liu.
